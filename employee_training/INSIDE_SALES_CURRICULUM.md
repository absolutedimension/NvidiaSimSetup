## 1b. TWO FLOWS — she works both, in opposite directions

**FLOW A — after Rohan (follow-up).** He visits; she picks it up by phone within 24–48h and carries
it to a first test.

**FLOW B — before Rohan (lead generation). ⭐ The priority.** Deepak hands her a **filtered list of
Patna institutes with details**. She calls them first, qualifies, and **books a demo appointment**
for Rohan at a slot the owner picks. She is filtering the list so **Rohan only travels to institutes
worth the trip**.

**Why Flow B matters more than it looks:** Rohan's single biggest loss is arriving when the owner has
no time — *"us waqt time kam tha unke paas"*, *"khare khare baat hua, dikha nahi paye"*. A booked slot
removes exactly that. His own curriculum already says **appointment-first beats cold-walking**; she is
what finally makes that possible.

### Flow B — her deliverable is a BOOKED SLOT, not a good conversation

Every call ends in one of three ways. Nothing vague:

1. **A date and time** for Rohan's demo
2. **A clear "not interested"** — logged with the reason, and she moves on
3. **A specific call-back time** — not "main baad mein call karungi"

**What she must capture before booking:** institute name · area · owner's name · rough student count ·
which exams/classes · **the pain they named** · the agreed slot. Rohan walking in already knowing all
of that is a completely different visit from a cold knock.

### ⚠️ Qualify BEFORE booking — protecting his day is the job

Do not send him across Patna for a 6-student tuition. Book only if **3 of 4** are true: 10+ students ·
teaches an exam-prep subject · named a real pain about test papers · she is speaking to the decision-maker.

Not qualified → **don't book**, log the reason. **A booking she shouldn't have made costs Rohan half a
day and costs her his trust in the whole list** — which is far more expensive than one missed meeting.

She needs the same product mastery Rohan has — she'll be asked the same questions, without a

## MODULE 2 — The call itself (her core skill, ~6 concepts)

**2.1 The 20-second open** — who you are, why you're calling, and why it's worth 2 minutes — before
he decides you're a telecaller. *Trap:* a long polite preamble; he's gone by second 15.

**2.2 Getting to the decision-maker** — reaching the owner, not the receptionist, without being rude
to the receptionist (who decides whether you ever get through). *Mastery:* three ways to ask for the
owner. *Trap:* pitching the person who answers because it's easier than asking for the boss.

**2.3 "Abhi busy hoon"** — the most common response. It's not a no, it's a bad moment. Book a
specific time instead of "main baad mein call karungi". *Mastery:* turn a brush-off into a slot.

**2.3b The qualifying call (Flow B)** — call a listed institute cold, get to the owner, ask the three
pain questions, decide in 2 minutes whether it's worth Rohan's trip, and book a specific slot.
*Mastery:* run one end-to-end with Maya. *Trap:* booking a friendly-but-unqualified institute because
the call went nicely.

**2.4 Warm pick-up after Rohan's visit** — she reads the ledger first, references what Rohan showed
and what the owner said, and **never makes him repeat himself**. That single detail is what makes
the team feel like a company rather than two people calling the same number.

**2.5 Always end with a commitment** — a specific next step with a date, agreed out loud. *Trap:*
"achhi baat hui" with nothing agreed — a call that felt good and moved nothing.

**2.6 The trial clock** — a 14-day trial is a deadline to manage warmly, never as pressure. Day 1–2:
get one test run. Day 10–12: confirm real usage, then tell Deepak. *Trap:* an expired trial nobody used.

---

## MODULE 3 — Keeping it alive (the retention half)

**3.1 The registered-but-not-tested list** — **her single highest-value list, every single day.**
These institutes said yes and are one nudge from being real. Work them before anything else.

**3.2 Making usage visible** — *"aaj aapke 12 students ne test diya, aur teen ko trigonometry mein
dikkat hai."* Real numbers from their own students are more persuasive than any pitch.

**3.3 The honest check-in** — asking "kya kaam nahi kar raha?" and actually reporting it back. Bugs
found this way are worth more than a compliment.

**3.4 Knowing when to stop** — two hard no's with a real reason, or three cold contacts: log it and
move on. **A clean no frees a day.** *Trap:* chasing the polite ones because they're pleasant, while
the real prospects go cold.

## MODULE 5 — Working with Rohan (the team half)

**5.1 One ledger, one truth** — `~/.openclaw/team/institutes.json`, shared. Both Saathis read and
write it. **Check it before contacting anyone.** *Trap:* calling an institute Rohan visited this
morning — the owner notices, and it looks disorganised.

**5.2 The handoff, both directions** — Rohan → Heena after every visit (who he met, the pain, what
he left, what's next). Heena → Rohan when an institute needs a person in the room again.

**5.3 Never contradict each other** — on price, on timelines, on what the product does. If unsure,
"main confirm karke batati hoon" beats a guess. One wrong number from either of them costs the
institute's trust in both.
