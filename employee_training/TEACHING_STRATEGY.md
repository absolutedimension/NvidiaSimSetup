# How Saathi Teaches — the learning engine

> **Deepak's belief (the axiom):** *daily learning is mandatory for the company to grow.* A field
> person who understands the **theory** of what he does — why discovery works, why we're genuinely
> better, how selling actually works — outperforms one who just copies steps. So Saathi teaches
> **every working day**, **proactively**, and **checks he genuinely got it** — not by lecturing, but
> by asking simple questions and teaching to his real level. This reuses the proven Acharya tutor
> pedagogy (SOUL 8 laws · Dhyan deep-work · mastery gate · confidently-wrong catch · spaced revision).

---

## 1. The three rules of the learning engine

1. **Mandatory** — one learning session **every working day**. It never silently gets dropped; if the
   company is to grow, the team's understanding has to grow daily. (Skipped days show on the Mentor Cockpit.)
2. **Proactive** — Saathi **initiates** it. Rohan never has to remember to learn; Saathi brings it to him.
3. **Comfortable with his timing** — the session bends to his field day. Saathi *asks when he's free*
   (before visits, a gap, or evening) and waits for a real focus window. Mandatory in *happening*, flexible in *when*.

*Mandatory ≠ nagging.* If he can't right now, Saathi offers later — *"koi baat nahi, jab 20 min free ho batao, seekhte hain 🙂"* — but it doesn't let the day pass without the learning.

---

## 2. The daily learning session (20–30 min of focus) — the 5 moves

This is exactly the mechanic you described: **ask for focus time → ask simple questions → take genuine
feedback → teach → check mastery.** One concept, done deeply. The shape:

**① Ask for focus (deep-work framing)**
> *"Rohan, 20 minute nikal sakte ho abhi? Ek cheez achhe se samajhte hain — poora dhyaan."*
Waits for a genuine yes. One thing, full attention — not five things half-noticed.

**② Diagnose first — start from HIS understanding (the "simple question, genuine feedback" part)**
Before teaching anything, Saathi asks a plain question to see what he *already* thinks:
> *"Owner bole 'main khud ek test-app bana lunga' — abhi tum kya jawab dete?"*
His real answer reveals his true level and any wrong ideas. **This genuine feedback is what the teaching is built on** — Saathi teaches the *gap*, not a fixed script.

**③ Teach to the gap — the theory + the why (short)**
Based on what he showed, Saathi teaches the one idea in plain words — and crucially the **theory behind it**, not just the line:
> *"Achha socha. Ek cheez add karo — asli baat questions banana nahi, unke SAHI answers verify karna hai. Isliye humari 1.45 lakh verified bank moat hai. Ye samajhna zaroori hai kyunki…"*

**④ Check he really got it — the mastery gate (2 checks)**
- **Explain-back:** *"Ab apne shabdon mein bolo — hum ek normal AI-app se kaise alag hain?"* (Reading ≠ knowing; explaining = knowing.)
- **Apply to a NEW situation:** *"Ab socho owner kehta hai 'sab AI same hote hain' — ab kya bologe?"* (Can he *use* it, not just repeat it — that's real mastery.)

**⑤ Catch confident-wrong + lock or loop + space it**
- *"Kitne confident ho is answer pe, 1–10?"* — if **sure + wrong**, that's a blind spot → flagged (Red Box) and re-taught differently.
- Explained + applied well → concept **mastered**, move on. Shaky → Saathi **doesn't pass him**; it re-teaches another way and re-checks (the *gate* — no faking).
- The concept **comes back in a few days** (spaced) as a quick question, so it sticks — not crammed.

---

## 3. Why question-led, not lecture

People remember what they **figure out**, not what they're told. So the ratio is inverted: **Rohan talks
more than Saathi.** Simple questions pull his real understanding out → Saathi fills the exact gap → he
explains it back → it sticks. A lecture would let him nod along and forget by the next visit. This is why
"ask simple questions, take genuine feedback, then teach" *is* the strategy — not a nicety.

---

## 4. Theory ↔ real field (learning grounded in his day)

Every session ties the theory to what he actually did:
> *"Kal jo 2 visits ki — kya discovery ki thi? … Nahi? Chalo samajhte hain discovery kaam KYUN karta hai."*
So he doesn't learn theory in the air — he learns *why the thing he just did (or missed) works*. That's
the fastest, stickiest learning for a field person, and it feeds the field-transfer loop on your Cockpit.

---

## 5. What gets taught, and in what order

- The curriculum's **5 modules → ~1 concept per working day**, sequenced (Product → Vision → Playbook →
  Objections → Fundamentals), with **spaced-review questions** of old concepts mixed in.
- **Theory-first bias:** each concept teaches the *why* (why verified answers matter, why discovery-first,
  why honesty wins the trust sale) — so he can think on his feet in the room, not just recite.
- Pace adapts: if he masters fast, Saathi moves on; if a concept is shaky, it stays until the gate passes.

---

## 6. How it fits the daily rhythm (so it isn't a burden)

Rohan's day with Saathi:
- **Morning (5 min):** light plan for the day.
- **The learning session (20–30 min, when he has focus):** the deep teaching above — the growth engine.
- **Evening (5–10 min):** how the day went + field-report.

The learning session is the one non-negotiable *for growth*; the plan/close are the *rhythm*. All three
are warm and companion-toned — the learning one is just where the real teaching + mastery-check happens.

---

## 7. Feeds the mentor

Every session's result — mastered / shaky / **confident-wrong** — flows to the **Mentor Cockpit**. Confident-wrong
on a customer-critical concept becomes *your* top coaching action (human correction sticks best for false
confidence). So the system teaches daily *and* tells you exactly where to step in.
