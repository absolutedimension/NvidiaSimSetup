## Read this before you leave the house

> **Lead with TEST PAPERS, not "AI tutor."** Acharya's one-line pitch is:
> *"Unlimited exam-authentic test papers, in your institute's name, in 30 seconds."*
> The tutor, the doubt-solving, the batch dashboard — those come later, only if they ask.

> **First 10 minutes are THEM talking.** You are diagnosing how their institute runs. The pitch
> comes only after you can repeat their problem back in their own words. A pitch without
> discovery is a pamphlet with a voice. **This applies to the warm three as well** — a booked
> demo is not a diagnosis.

**Pitch only if 3 of these 4 are true** (tick them on the card):

- [ ] ≥10 current paying students
- [ ] Teaches an exam-prep subject (board / NEET / JEE / govt-exam)
- [ ] Named a real pain when you asked about test papers
- [ ] The person in front of you owns the money decision

If not qualified: thank them, ask **"who do you know who runs a bigger batch?"**, log it, leave.
A polite no from the wrong person teaches nothing.

### The three discovery questions that matter for test series

1. **Who writes your test papers right now, and how long does one paper take?**
2. **How often do you run a test — and do you ever skip one because the paper isn't ready?**
3. **After a test, how do you find out which chapter the whole batch is weak in?**

Listen for the one that makes them talk fast. That's the pain you sell to.

### About the warm three — know this before you knock

Stops 1, 2 and 5 were already called by **Maya, our AI phone assistant, calling as "Acharya."**
They spoke to her on the phone and **agreed to a demo.** So:

- Open with **"Maine aapko Acharya se call kiya tha — aap demo ke liye maan gaye the"** —
  it's a follow-through, not a cold knock.
- If they don't remember the call, don't argue and don't explain that it was an AI. Just say
  *"koi baat nahi sir, 10 minute me main dikha deta hoon"* and run the normal discovery.
- **All three are JEE/NEET** — our question bank for those is live and tested, so **you can
  and should run a real demo paper on your phone.** Don't leave without showing it.

# DAY 3

## How Day 3 is different — read this first

Days 1 and 2 were institutes we found from their websites. **Day 3's four all run their own
branded mobile app** — which means the owner has already paid real money for software (these
apps cost lakhs a year). Two things follow:

- **They are not scared of technology and they have a budget.** You are not convincing them to
  start spending. You are asking them to spend on the part their app is missing.
- **Their app has a test section with no questions in it.** The app company sold them the
  *tool*; nobody gave them the *papers*. That hole is the whole conversation.

**So the opening question for Day 3 is different:**

> "Sir, aapka apna app hai — usme test section bhi hai. **Us test section me questions kaun
> daalta hai?**"

Nine times out of ten the answer is "hum khud" or "abhi khaali hai." That's your opening.

# After every visit — 5 minutes, non-negotiable

Same day, message Deepak these four lines per institute. This is the real output of the day —
more valuable than a yes.

1. **Outcome** — pilot booked / follow-up (date) / no / unqualified-referral
2. **Their pain, verbatim** — one line, their words
3. **Their objection, verbatim** — one line, their words
4. **Who writes their papers today** — a person's name, a vendor, or "nobody, we skip tests"

Plus, for the warm three: **did they remember the Acharya call?** Yes/no. That tells us whether
Maya's bookings are real.

## Do / Don't

**DO** phone ahead the morning of the visit · ask for the owner by name at the desk · write
their words down as they say them · correct this sheet where it's wrong · run the live demo at
stops 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 9, 10 · ask every "no" for one referral.

**DON'T** pitch before discovery · demo commerce papers at Stop 8 · read the bad reviews out loud
at Stop 12 · pitch at Stop 11 before confirming the app is theirs · explain that Maya is an AI ·
quote a price Deepak hasn't confirmed · leave without either a booked pilot, a dated follow-up,
or a referral name.

For Day 3 add one extra line to your evening message: **does their app's test section actually
have questions in it — yes, empty, or "hum khud banate hain"?** That single answer tells us
whether this whole batch of leads is worth repeating in other cities.
