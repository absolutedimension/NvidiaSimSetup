# The Revisit Ladder — why going back is the job, not the failure

**For:** Rohan (Patna) · Priyanshu (Ara & Bhojpur) · **Issued:** 2026-08-19

---

## The thing you've heard, and what's actually true

You may have heard: *"a person needs to see you 7 times before they trust you."*

**The number 7 is made up.** It comes from Hollywood studio advertising in the 1930s — someone said it,
everyone repeated it, and it started sounding like a law. No study has ever established 7 as the
magic number.

**But the idea underneath it is real, and it's one of the most tested findings in psychology:**

- **Mere-exposure effect** — people like a person or thing MORE simply because they have seen it before.
  Not because of new information. Just familiarity. Proven again and again since 1968.
- **Processing fluency** — a familiar face is easier for the brain to handle, and the brain misreads
  that ease as *"I trust this person."*
- **Illusory truth** — a message heard several times gets judged as more true than the same message
  heard once.

So: **repetition genuinely builds trust. The number is invented.** Some owners say yes on visit two.
Some take six months. There is no counter that runs out.

---

## The three catches — read these, they matter more than the rule

**1. Repetition multiplies the FIRST impression, it doesn't erase it.**
Mere-exposure only works when the first meeting was neutral or good. If your first visit annoyed
someone, coming back makes it **worse**, not better. This is why "bas baar-baar jaate raho" is bad
advice on its own.

**2. Too much becomes irritation.** There's a ceiling. The same pitch a fifth time doesn't build
trust, it builds resistance.

**3. Trust is not need, and trust is not budget.** An owner can genuinely like you and still not buy,
because he has no test-paper problem or no money this month. Familiarity removes friction. It does
not create a reason.

### The one rule that handles all three

> ## Never revisit empty-handed.
>
> Repeating the same **ask** is pestering. Repeating with something **new** is a relationship.

That single line is the whole difference between a salesman owners avoid and one they make time for.

---

## Why going back feels bad (and why the feeling is wrong)

Going back feels like begging. That feeling comes from believing the first "no" was a **decision
about you**.

It almost never is. On a first visit the owner is busy, mid-class, has never heard of us, and has no
reason yet. **"Abhi nahi" is not a rejection — it's an owner who hasn't been given a reason.**

You are not going back to ask again. **You are going back to bring the next thing.** That is a
completely different act, and owners can feel the difference immediately.

A real one only comes at visit 4 or 5, when he has seen the product, held a paper made for his batch,
and been invited to try it with his own students. **Anything before that is not a no — it's just "not yet".**

---

## 💬 Price you CAN say. Money you never ask for.

**It starts on a 14-day FREE TRIAL — so there is no payment conversation at this stage at all.**

Your ask is always the same: *"free trial shuru kar lijiye."* Never *"kitna doge."*

| You DO | You DON'T |
|---|---|
| State the price openly if he asks | Ask for money |
| Say plainly what's free and for how long | Negotiate or offer a discount |
| Get them registered and testing | Chase a payment |
| Say Deepak will connect after the trial | Try to close |

**Say the price straight when asked.** Dodging it makes you look like you're hiding something and
kills trust faster than any number will. Being clear costs you nothing — nobody is paying today.

**If an owner pushes to talk terms:** give him the honest price, then —
*"Sir, abhi 14 din bilkul free hai. Pehle chala ke dekh lijiye apne students pe. Uske baad Deepak
sir aapse baat kar lenge."* Then get back to setting up the test.

**Your win is an institute actually USING it on the trial** — even 5 students, even one batch.
Whether they pay afterwards is the owner's call and Deepak's conversation, not your pressure to carry.

That answer is honest, it removes the pressure from the room, and it's how you get to a real yes —
because an owner who has already seen his own students use it is a completely different
conversation from one who has only heard a pitch.

## THE LADDER — every visit has its own job

Never "just following up". Every visit has a purpose and one thing you bring.

| # | Visit | Your ONE job | What you carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Introduce + diagnose** | Find the pain. **Do NOT pitch.** Ask the 3 questions and listen. | Visiting card |
| **2** | **Show** | 60-second live demo — **their** subject, on your phone | Phone with app + a test already made |
| **3** | **Give** | Hand over a free printed paper **made for their batch**. Ask for nothing. | The printed paper + answer key |
| **4** | **Register + first test** | Get them **signed up and a test actually run** — even 5–10 students is a win | Phone; do it WITH him, on the spot |
| **5** | **Keep it running** | Help them use it for real — a second test, another batch, the weak-topic report | Their results, printed |
| **6** | **Report usage** | Tell Deepak it's live and being used. **Deepak takes it from there.** | The usage numbers |
| **7+** | **Stay warm** | Once a month, always with something new | New subject, new feature, a result |

**Visit 3 is the one people skip, and it's the most powerful.** Giving something real, made for
*their* students, with no ask attached, changes the relationship more than any pitch. Owners remember
who gave them something free that was actually useful.

**Only ONE ask per visit.** Two asks in one visit reads as desperation.

---

## Do NOT climb this ladder with everyone

The ladder costs you a whole day per institute. Spend it only where it can pay.

Climb it only if **3 of these 4** are true (the qualify check):

- [ ] 10+ paying students
- [ ] Teaches an exam-prep subject
- [ ] Named a real pain about test papers
- [ ] The person you're meeting owns the money decision

**Not qualified → stop at visit 1.** Thank them, ask *"aapke jaan-pehchaan mein koi bada batch chalata
hai?"*, log it, move on. Six visits to an institute with 6 students is six wasted days.

**Also stop if:** he's said a hard no twice with a real reason, or he's gone cold for 3 visits with no
engagement. Log it honestly and move on. **A clean no frees a day.**

## The short version

1. The "7 times" number is fake. **Repetition building trust is real.**
2. **Never go empty-handed.** Same ask = pestering. New thing = relationship.
3. **Early "no" is not a no.** A real answer only exists at visit 5–6.
4. **One ask per visit.**
5. **Qualify first.** Don't climb the ladder with an institute too small to matter.
6. **Say the price if asked; never ask for the money.** 14 days free — your win is them TESTING it.
7. Ask yourself: *which visit is this, and what am I taking?*
