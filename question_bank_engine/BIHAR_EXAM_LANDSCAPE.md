## §0. The mental model — Bihar recruits through 12 bodies, not one

This is the thing nobody explains to a candidate (or to an institute owner). "Bihar
government job" is not one exam system. It is **twelve separate conducting
authorities**, split by the *rank* and *nature* of the post — not by department.

| Tier | What it covers | Who conducts |
|---|---|---|
| **Gazetted / Class-I & II** | SDO, DSP, Revenue Officer, Judges, APO, Assistant Engineer, Assistant Professor, Headmaster | **BPSC** |
| **Class-III non-gazetted (clerical/ministerial)** | Clerk, ASO, Data Entry Operator, Auditor, Panchayat Sachiv, Office Attendant | **BSSC** |
| **Police — officer cadre** | Sub-Inspector, Sergeant, ASI, Enforcement SI, Excise SI | **BPSSC** |
| **Police — constabulary** | Constable, Driver, Warder, Fireman, Forest Guard, Home Guard | **CSBC** |
| **Technical & medical** | Junior Engineer, ANM, Staff Nurse, Pharmacist, Lab Technician, Dresser | **BTSC** |
| **School teaching** | Teacher (TRE), Head Teacher, Headmaster, Special Teacher | **BPSC** (exam) + **BSEB** (eligibility: TET/STET) |
| **Entrance & merit-listing** | ITI, Diploma, Paramedical, and the Amin/Kanungo land-survey cadre | **BCECEB** |
| **PSUs & corporations** | Power (BSPHCL), IT (BELTRON), Cooperative Banks (BSCB) | each PSU itself |
| **Health mission (contract)** | Community Health Officer, ANM on contract | **SHSB** |
| **Legislature secretariat** | ASO, Reporter, Junior Clerk, Security Guard | **Vidhan Sabha / Parishad** |
| **Judiciary staff** | Civil Court Clerk, Stenographer, Reader, Peon | **Patna High Court / district courts** |
| **Board exams & eligibility** | STET, D.El.Ed, Simultala entrance | **BSEB** |

**The commercially important consequence** (see §11): the first six tiers cover
~95% of the candidate volume, and **five of them test the same four subject
buckets**. One well-built bank serves many exams.

## §2. BPSC in depth

### 2.1 Combined Competitive Examination (CCE) — the flagship

**Prelims** — one paper, screening only, marks not carried forward.

| | |
|---|---|
| Paper | General Studies |
| Questions | 150 |
| Marks | 150 |
| Duration | 2 hours |
| Negative marking | **−1/3** per wrong answer |

*Prelims syllabus:* General Science (everyday observation, no specialisation
assumed) · Current events, national and international · History of India **and
Bihar** — with explicit weight on the 19th-century resurgence and the freedom
movement in Bihar · Geography — physical, social, economic, and Bihar's
agriculture and natural resources · Indian Polity, Panchayati Raj, community
development, Five-Year Plans · Indian and **Bihar** economy post-Independence ·
Mental ability / reasoning.

**Mains — ⚠️ the pattern changed and most coaching sites are still wrong.**

| Paper | Subject | Marks | Counts toward merit? |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | General Hindi | 100 | **No** — qualifying, 30% needed |
| II | General Studies I | 300 | Yes |
| III | General Studies II | 300 | Yes |
| IV | **Essay** | 300 | Yes |
| V | Optional subject | 300 | **No — now objective-type and qualifying only** |

**Merit total = 900 (GS-I + GS-II + Essay) + 120 interview = 1,020.**

The optional subject *used to* carry merit, and dozens of sites still publish "34
optional subjects, mains total 1,050." That is stale. From the 72nd CCE the
optional paper is objective and qualifying. If you are building a BPSC product,
this single fact reorders the whole preparation strategy — and it is the kind of
thing an institute owner will test you on.

*GS-I:* Indian culture, modern Indian history with Bihar emphasis, contemporary
events, statistical analysis and diagram/graph interpretation.
*GS-II:* Indian and Bihar polity, economy, geography; the role of science and
technology in development.

**Stages:** Prelims → Mains → Interview (120). Eligibility: bachelor's degree;
age as of 1 Aug of the exam year — UR male 37, UR female 40, BC/EBC 40, SC/ST 42.

**2026 status:** 72nd prelims was set for 26 Jul 2026, **postponed**, now
tentatively **25 Oct 2026**.

### 2.2 Bihar Judicial Services (33rd) — 173 posts
LL.B. required. Prelims (objective) → Mains (descriptive, law papers) →
Interview. Conducted bilingually. This is a distinct, high-ticket coaching
market with almost no overlap with the GS bank.

### 2.3 Assistant Prosecution Officer (APO) — 300 posts, Advt. 13/2026
LL.B.; age 21–37/40/42 by category as of 01-08-2026. Prelims → Mains →
Interview, bilingual. Core: criminal law, evidence law, constitutional law.

### 2.4 Assistant Engineer (AE) — 1,040 posts
Six papers, ~600 questions, ~600 marks, 6 hours total: General Studies, General
English, General Hindi, General Engineering, plus **two discipline papers**
(Civil / Mechanical / Electrical). Four common + two branch-specific.

### 2.5 TRE — Teacher Recruitment Examination (the volume monster)

**TRE-4.0: 46,595 vacancies per the BPSC calendar** (other reporting says
32,388 — ⚠️ verify). Exam **22–27 Sep 2026**.

| | |
|---|---|
| Questions | 150 |
| Marks | 150 |
| Duration | 2 h 30 m |
| Negative marking | **None in the Language section; applicable in General Studies** |

| Level | Paper structure |
|---|---|
| Classes 1–5 (PRT) | Language + General Studies |
| Classes 6–8 | Language + General Studies + **concerned subject** |
| Classes 9–10 (TGT) | Language + General Studies + **concerned subject** |
| Classes 11–12 (PGT) | Language + General Studies + **concerned subject** |

*Language section:* English **plus one** of Hindi / Urdu / Bangla.
*General Studies (primary level):* elementary mathematics, mental ability,
general awareness, general science, social science, Indian national movement,
geography, environment.

**Prerequisite:** BSEB's **TET** (classes 1–8) or **STET** (9–12). No TET/STET,
no TRE. This is the single most-asked question in a Bihar institute.

| | Head Teacher | Headmaster |

| Duration | — | 2 hours |
| Sections | General Studies 75 + **D.El.Ed** 75 | General Studies 100 + **B.Ed** 50 |

## §3. BSSC in depth

### 3.1 Inter Level (10+2) — 26,426 posts, the largest single clerical exam

**Prelims** — CBT, 2 h 15 m, **150 questions / 600 marks**, **+4 / −1**.

| Section | Q | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| General Studies | 50 | 200 |
| General Science & Mathematics | 50 | 200 |
| Mental Ability / Logical Reasoning | 50 | 200 |

**Mains** — two papers, 2 h 15 m each:
- **Paper 1:** General Awareness / **Hindi Language** — 100 Q, 400 marks
- **Paper 2:** Mental ability + reasoning + mathematics + science — 150 Q, 600 marks

Then **document verification → skill/typing test**. Qualifying marks run 32–40%
by category.

### 3.2 CGL / Graduate Level — same architecture, graduate difficulty

**Prelims** — 2 h 15 m, 150 Q / 600 marks, **+4 / −1**, three 50-question
sections: General Studies · General Science & Mathematics ·
Comprehension / Logical Reasoning / Mental Ability. **Qualifying only** — marks
do not enter the final merit.

**Mains** — Paper 1 **Hindi, 100 Q / 400 marks (30% minimum to qualify)**;
Paper 2 = GS (200) + General Science & Maths (200) + Reasoning (200) = 600.
**No interview.** Final merit is Mains alone.

*Distinctive content:* Bihar history and culture is a named prelims topic, and
General Studies explicitly covers constitutional bodies (Election Commission,
UPSC), environment, and Bihar-specific economy.
*Hindi paper topics:* समास, संधि, अलंकार, spelling, vocabulary, sentence
correction — this is formal Hindi grammar, not comprehension, and it is where
English-medium candidates lose the exam.

### 3.3 Panchayat Sachiv — 3,532 posts
10+2 + computer proficiency (Hindi and English typing, MS Office). Age 18–37.
Prelims and Mains both 2 h 15 m with −1 negative marking. Same subject spine as
Inter Level.

## §4. BPSSC — Police Sub-Inspector

| Stage | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Prelims** | 100 questions, **200 marks**, **−0.2** per wrong answer. Qualifying. |
| **Mains** | Paper 1 **General Hindi (200)** + Paper 2 **General Studies (200)**. Must qualify both. |
| **PET** | Candidates up to **6× vacancies** called. Running, high jump, long jump, shot put. |
| **PST** | Height, chest, weight. **Qualifying only, no marks.** |
| Then | Document verification → medical |

*Prelims syllabus:* General Knowledge, Current Affairs, General Science, History,
Geography, Indian Polity, Indian Economy, Mathematics, Mental Ability, Reasoning.
*Mains:* General Hindi + General Studies, General Science, Civics, Indian History,
Indian Geography, Mathematics, Mental Ability.

Note the Hindi paper is **200 marks and merit-bearing** here — heavier than in
most other Bihar exams.

## §5. CSBC — Constabulary

**Written:** ~100 objective questions. For **Constable**, 100 marks / 2 hours at
**BSEB Class-10 (matric) standard**. For **Forest Guard**, 100 questions × 4 marks
= **400 marks** / 2 hours at 10+2 standard.

*Subjects:* Hindi, English, Mathematics, General Knowledge & Current Affairs,
General Science (Physics/Chemistry/Biology), Social Science.

**Then PMT/PST (height, chest) → PET (running, long jump, shot put) → document
verification → medical.** The written exam is only a gate; **physical performance
decides selection**, which is why constable coaching is a different business from
clerical coaching.

**Home Guard is the outlier: no written exam at all.** PST → PET (15 marks,
1,600 m in 6 minutes, high jump, long jump, shot put) → medical → DV. ~13,500
posts. There is no paper to sell here.

## §7. Cross-cutting rules (these apply almost everywhere)

**Age (upper limit, typical state pattern, reckoned on a stated cut-off date):**
UR male 37 · UR female 40 · BC/EBC 40 · SC/ST 42. Constabulary and Forest Guard
run much younger (Forest Guard ≤23).

**Vertical reservation:** SC, ST, EBC, BC, EWS.
**Horizontal reservation:** **35% for women**, applied across all categories —
and since **8 July 2025, restricted to Bihar-domiciled women only**. This was a
real policy change and it altered the arithmetic for a large cohort. Also
horizontal: PwBD.

**Documents that gate everything:** domicile (आवासीय) certificate, caste
certificate, non-creamy-layer certificate where applicable.

**Language:** most exams are bilingual, but **Hindi is not merely a medium — it is
a scored subject** in BPSC Mains (qualifying), BSSC CGL Mains (400 marks),
BSSC Inter Mains, BPSSC SI Mains (200 marks), Civil Court, and Constable. Formal
Hindi grammar (समास, संधि, अलंकार, वाक्य शुद्धि) is a recurring, coachable, and
badly-served block.

## §8. The pattern underneath all of it

Strip away the branding and **the entire non-technical Bihar exam surface tests
four buckets plus Hindi**:

1. **General Studies** — Indian history/polity/economy/geography **+ an explicit
   Bihar layer** (Bihar history, Bihar's 19th-century resurgence, Bihar's freedom
   movement, Bihar economy, Bihar geography and agriculture)
2. **General Science** — Physics, Chemistry, Biology at matric/inter standard
3. **Mathematics** — arithmetic, percentage, time & work, profit/loss, interest,
   ratio, mensuration, data interpretation
4. **Reasoning / Mental Ability** — series, coding-decoding, syllogism, seating,
   Venn, blood relations
5. **General Hindi** — formal grammar

The exams that draw on **exactly this spine**: BPSC CCE Prelims · BSSC CGL
(both stages) · BSSC Inter Level (both stages) · BSSC Panchayat Sachiv · BPSSC
Sub-Inspector (both stages) · CSBC Constable · CSBC Forest Guard · Bihar Civil
Court · Vidhan Sabha clerical · BPSC TRE General Studies section.

Add up their live vacancies: **Inter Level 26,426 + Constable 19,838 + TRE
32k–46k + Warder 2,417 + Forest Guard 1,816 + Prohibition 1,685 + Panchayat
Sachiv 3,532 + SI ~1,799 + CGL ~1,883 + Driver 4,361** — that is **north of
95,000 posts against one shared subject spine**, all within the 2026 calendar.

**The Bihar-specific layer is the moat.** National GS banks do not carry Bihar
history, Bihar polity, Bihar economy, or Bihar geography at exam depth. That
content only exists inside Bihar's own past papers — which is precisely what the
`exact-question-making-pipeline-from-pdf` route produces, and precisely what a
Patna institute cannot buy from a Delhi publisher.

**What is NOT servable by that spine** (do not over-promise these to an institute):
Judicial Services and APO (pure law) · Assistant Engineer and BSPHCL JE
(discipline engineering) · BTSC nursing/paramedical (clinical) · STET subject
papers (34 separate subjects) · Home Guard and DLRS Amin (no written exam at all).
