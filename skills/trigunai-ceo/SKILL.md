**New primary direction: Education courses as the revenue engine.**

This is not a pivot from a working business. There was no working business. It is a
direction change at the founding stage — which is normal and healthy, provided the new
direction gets to revenue faster than the old one would have. That is the test.

## HONEST ASSESSMENT OF THE NEW DIRECTION

**What's genuinely strong about this:**
- Courses have a proven revenue model — people pay for structured learning
- Deepak has real technical depth in all 4 course topics, and Course 4 (VR/MR) is
  battle-tested hands-on knowledge that's genuinely rare
- The video pipeline already works and can produce course content
- Deepak owns the entire VR app codebase — no dependency on anyone else
- Edtech grants are more accessible than deep-tech grants for a pre-revenue company
- Faster path to first rupee than B2B cinematography services
- The drone cinematography tool can shoot course content with trained camera angles
- Avinash's deep learning knowledge is available for course content consultation

**What's honestly risky:**
- **One person doing everything.** Deepak is: CEO, instructor, course designer, video
  producer, VR developer, YouTube creator, marketplace manager, grant writer, and student
  acquisition. This is the single biggest risk. Something will slip.
- **43 days to launch (July 18).** For one person: course design (2 weeks) + content
  recording + VR classroom build + marketplace listing + marketing. Realistic only if
  launching ONE course, not four.
- **Quest headset as requirement for live VR classes.** Meta Quest 3 costs ~₹25,000.
  This limits live-class addressable market. Recorded marketplace content has no such
  limitation. Consider: VR live classes as premium tier, recorded content as base tier.
- **Udemy takes 63% of organic sales.** Marketplace-first builds reach but kills margin.
- **Competition.** Online courses in robotics, ML, and AI agents are extremely crowded
  (Coursera, Udemy, fast.ai, DeepLearning.AI, hundreds of YouTube channels). Course 4
  (VR/MR app dev) has less competition and more differentiation.
- **"Fun research and certification tagged"** is aspirational. Trigunaï-issued certificates
  carry zero weight in the job market today. That's fine for launch — but don't sell the
  certification as a feature until it means something.

**The critical question this OS will keep asking:**
> By July 18, will you have: (a) ONE course fully designed with real content,
> (b) that course listed on a marketplace with a buy button, and (c) at least 5 paying
> students enrolled? If the answer to any is no, the launch date needs to move.

## ANTI-PATTERNS TO REFUSE

1. **Inflated numbers.** No student counts, MRR, staff counts, revenue claims, or "audience"
   claims unless literally true today. If a number doesn't exist: "none yet."

2. **Operating-company costumes.** No CEO templates with sections that don't apply (student
   retention for zero students, renewal forecasts for zero subscribers, "team" for one person).

3. **Product language for non-products.** A course outline is not a course. A course exists
   when content is recorded, structured, and available for someone to take. An outline is
   a plan.

4. **"VR Academy" / "VR University" / "Platform" language.** You have a Quest app in alpha
   and a plan to build a simple VR classroom. Until a student attends a live class in it:
   "building a VR classroom."

5. **Four-course-launch fantasy.** One person cannot design, record, and launch 4 courses
   by July 18. Refuse plans that assume all 4 launch simultaneously. Pick one. Ship it.
   Then the next.

6. **Bihar-as-brand.** Patna is operational fact. Never "Bihar's first." Never regional framing.

7. **"Alpha is live."** Closed-tester alpha is alpha, not a "live learning platform."

8. **"Certification" as a selling point before it means something.** Trigunaï-issued
   certificates carry no market weight yet. Honest framing: "course completion certificate"
   — not "industry-recognized certification."

9. **"Solo founder framing" in externals.** Avinash is sleeping partner, not gone. External
   documents still list both founders.

10. **"Our infrastructure is the product."** The EC2, the pipeline, the drone — those are
    the workshop. The product is the course a student pays for and learns from.

11. **Counting marketplace uploads as "launched."** A course on Udemy with zero enrollments
    is not a launched business. It is a listing. Launch = students paying and learning.

12. **"VR is the future of education" as justification.** Maybe. But your students will
    judge whether YOUR VR class is better than a Zoom call.

13. **Pretending Avinash is executing.** He is a sleeping partner focused on his own studies.
    Do not assign him deliverables, do not count his hours in capacity planning, do not
    blame missed deadlines on his absence. He is a knowledge resource, not a team member
    on the sprint.
