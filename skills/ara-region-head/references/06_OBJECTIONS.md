# 06 · The nine objections — have the answer ready

> **The principle behind every answer below: agree first, then reframe.** Never argue with an
> institute owner in his own office. He is right about his world; he is just missing one fact about
> ours. Your job is to hand him that fact, not to win.
>
> **And the honest ones stay honest.** If the true answer is "we don't have that", say it. You are
> building a region for years, not closing one paper today.

---

## 1. *"Per student kitna lagega?"*

> **"Per student kuch nahi, sir. Flat hai — ₹250 mahina, chahe 50 students hon ya 500."**

Then stop talking. This is the single strongest thing in our pricing and it lands hardest in
silence. Most owners have been quoted per-head pricing and know the bill grows every time they grow.

---

## 2. *"AI se padhai nahi hoti / AI pe bharosa nahi."*

**Agree with him. Completely.**

> **"Bilkul sahi kaha sir. AI padha nahi sakta — padhana aapka kaam hai, wo koi nahi le sakta.
> Isliye hum padhane ki baat kar hi nahi rahe. Ye sirf PAPER banata hai — wo bhi naya nahi likhta,
> purane ASLI papers ke asli questions se. Aapka teen ghanta bachta hai, bas."**

This works because it is true, and because it shrinks the claim from something he distrusts (AI
teaching) to something mechanical he can verify on the spot (real past questions, assembled fast).

---

## 3. *"Mere paas already Classplus / app hai."*

> **"Bahut achha sir. Usme test banane ka option bhi hai na? Usme kitne questions bhare hue hain?"**

The answer is **none**. Classplus gives him an empty test module and he has to supply every question
himself. He is often paying lakhs for the privilege.

> **"To hum uska replacement nahi hain — hum us khali test module ko bharte hain. Aapka app chalta
> rahe, paper humse aayega."**

Never attack the platform he already bought. He chose it; attacking it makes him defend it.

---

## 4. *"Students phone nahi laate / phone allow nahi hai."*

> **"Bilkul, isliye paper PRINT hota hai — aapke naam se, aapke logo ke saath. Aap print kijiye,
> students paper pe test dein, jaise abhi dete hain. Phone ki zaroorat sirf aapko hai, paper
> nikalne ke liye."**

⚠️ In Ara this objection will be common and it is a **real** one, not a brush-off. The printable
paper is the answer, and it is genuinely how most Ara institutes will use us. **Carry a printed
sample paper** so this answer is something he can hold.

---

## 5. *"Abhi time nahi hai / baad mein aana."*

Do not accept a vague later. Shrink the ask:

> **"Sir do minute. Sirf ek cheez dikhata hoon — aap chapter chuniye, main paper nikaal ke dikhata
> hoon. Pasand na aaye to main nikal jaunga."**

If he genuinely can't: **book a specific time** — *"kal subah 11 baje theek hai?"* — and write it
down in front of him. "Baad mein" without a time is a no; take the no and log it.

---

## 6. *"Kitna paisa? ... Mehenga hai."*

Almost never a real objection at ₹250. What he is actually saying is *"I don't yet believe it's
worth anything."* So don't defend the price — remove it:

> **"Sir paisa abhi dena hi nahi hai. Ek mahina free chalaiye. Students use karenge to baat karenge,
> nahi karenge to band. Aapka kuch nahi jaata."**

If he still pushes on price, do the arithmetic out loud:
> **"Ek paper banane mein aapke teen ghante jaate hain. Mahine mein bara ghante. ₹250 mein."**

---

## 7. *"Ye questions asli hain? Kahan se laaye?"*

The best question anyone can ask you. Answer it straight:

> **"Asli past papers se, sir — TRE ke purane papers, unki official answer key ke saath. Do hazaar
> se zyada TRE questions. AI ne likhe nahi hain. Aap khud check kar lijiye — koi bhi question uthaiye
> aur apne guide se milaiye."**

**Then actually invite him to check one.** An owner who verifies a question against his own
guidebook and finds it correct is more than convinced — he becomes an advocate.

---

## 8. *"Aap log kaun hain? Kitne institute use kar rahe hain?"*

**Do not inflate. Ever.** He will check, and the town is small.

> **"Sir hum naye hain — TrigunAI, ek AI product company. Deepak sir khud product banate hain.
> Hum abhi apne pehle serious partners dhoondh rahe hain, aur isliye jo aap bologe wo seedha
> product mein jaayega. Bade company mein aapki baat sunne wala koi nahi hoga."**

In a small town, "the founder answers the phone" beats "10,000 institutes trust us" — because he has
been ignored by a big company before. **Make our smallness the offer.**

---

## 9. *"Sochkar batata hoon."*

This means the demo didn't land or he isn't the decision-maker. Diagnose, don't push:

> **"Bilkul sir. Bas ek baat — kya cheez hai jo abhi clear nahi hui?"**

Whatever he says next is the **real** objection; everything before it was politeness. Answer that
one. Then, instead of asking again:

> **"Ek kaam karte hain — aaj ek batch pe free laga dete hain. Aap sochte rahiye, students tab tak
> use karke dekh lenge."**

**And log what he said, verbatim.** A logged no with a real reason is a win for the company. An
unlogged maybe is nothing.

---

## The meta-rule

If you don't know an answer: **"Sir main confirm karke aaj shaam tak bata deta hoon."**

Nobody has ever lost a deal by checking. People lose deals by guessing, being wrong, and being found
out three weeks later when the institute is already using the product.
