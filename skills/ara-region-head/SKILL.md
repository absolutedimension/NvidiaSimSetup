## 2. Build the list (your first real task)

Your working list is `references/ara_institutes.csv`. Columns:
`name, cluster, subject, owner, phone, size_est, priority, precall, visit_status, pilot_status, pain_notes`

**It starts nearly empty on purpose. Filling it is your first week's work, and it is the single
highest-value thing you will do in the first 15 days** — the list *is* the region.

Ask your Claude: **"who should I work in <area> today?"** → it reads the CSV, filters your area,
ranks by priority, and hands you a walking route.

Where the names come from: `references/03_ARA_SOURCING_GUIDE.md`. Short version — **Ara is an
on-foot market.** The Play Store sweep that works in Patna found only two real Ara institutes with
apps. Google Maps per locality + your own eyes on signboards + your local network will beat any
scraper here. That is your home advantage; use it.

**The rule that shapes everything:** never cold-WhatsApp a number you scraped. Businesses publish
numbers to receive **calls**. So you call, or you walk in — and you get the WhatsApp opt-in *in the
room*. Bulk WhatsApp to scraped numbers risks our business number for everyone, including Rohan.

## 3. The visit (this is the job)

**Qualify before you pitch — 3 of these 4 must be true:**

- [ ] ≥10 current paying students
- [ ] Teaches an exam-prep subject (govt exam / board / NEET / JEE)
- [ ] Named a real pain when you asked about test papers
- [ ] The person in front of you owns the money decision

Not qualified? Thank them, ask *"aapke jaan-pehchaan mein koi bada batch chalata hai?"*, log it,
leave. A polite no from the wrong person teaches nothing and costs an hour.

**The order in the room — never invert this:**

1. **Discovery first — 10 minutes, they talk.** Full question set in
   `references/07_FIELD_RESEARCH_GUIDE.md`. The three that matter most for us:
   - *"Aapke test papers abhi kaun banata hai, aur ek paper mein kitna time lagta hai?"*
   - *"Kitni baar test hota hai — aur kabhi paper ready na hone se test skip hua hai?"*
   - *"Test ke baad aapko kaise pata chalta hai ki poora batch kis chapter mein kamzor hai?"*

   Listen for the one that makes them talk fast. **That** is the pain you sell to.

   **Plus one we specifically need answered from Ara** — the papers are English-only today:
   - *"Abhi paper English mein aata hai. Aapke students ko Hindi mein chahiye, ya English chal jayega?"*
   - then, always: *"Agar English hi rahe, to aap phir bhi use karenge — ya rukega?"*

   The second question is the one that matters. *"Hindi hota to achha hota"* is a wish; **"Hindi ke
   bina nahi chalega" is a requirement.** Log the exact words. Ara is a Hindi-medium govt-exam
   market, so your answer decides whether we build Hindi papers at all — see §5.
2. **Mirror the pain back** using `references/ACHARYA_PAINPOINT_MATCH.md` — let them recognise
   their own problem in a list, then ask *"aur koi jo is list mein nahi hai?"* That answer is gold.
3. **Demo — hand them the phone.** `references/04_DEMO_PLAYBOOK.md`. They pick the exam. They pick
   the chapter. You stay quiet.
4. **Don't end the demo — start the pilot.** *"Chaliye aaj hi aapke ek batch pe laga dete hain,
   aapke naam se. Free hai — paisa month ke baad."*
5. **Capture before you leave:** batch size · which exam · start date · **the name of the person who
   will run it daily**. That last one is the difference between a pilot and a nice conversation.

**Carry:** phone charged, demo tested that morning, the one-page Hindi leave-behind, and a pen. A
visit with no leave-behind is half-wasted.

## 4. What counts as progress (be strict with yourself)

The trap in this job is that a good conversation *feels* like a sale. It isn't.

| Real | Not real |
|---|---|
| A batch is live and students took a test this week | "Owner ko bahut pasand aaya" |
| The owner named who will run it daily | "Wo sochkar batayenge" |
| Money arrived | "Next month se shuru karenge" |
| A logged **no** with the exact reason | An un-logged maybe |

**Installed and in use** is the standard. Liking is free and predicts nothing. Deepak learned this
the expensive way — read the last line of `references/01_PRODUCT_MASTER.md`.

## 5. Log every visit the same day

No log = invisible = it did not happen (and, for your team, = unpaid).

Tell your Claude: **"log my visit to <institute>: <what happened>"** → it writes the row into
`references/ara_institutes.csv` (institute, area, date, outcome, pain in their words, pilot status,
next step) and appends the market-intelligence note.

**Log the NOs with their exact reason** — "too costly", "students phone nahi laate", "AI pe bharosa
nahi", "already have Classplus", "abhi time nahi". A no with a reason is worth more to Deepak than a
maybe. A pain heard 3+ times with a number attached becomes something we build.

Ask **"show my week"** any time for your own count.

## 6. Your daily loop — just say "start my day"

1. **"Who do I work in <area> today?"** → route for the day (§2)
2. Call ahead where you have a number; walk in where you don't
3. Visit → discovery → demo → free pilot (§3)
4. **Log everything same-day** (§5)
5. End of day: **"show my week"** + one line to Deepak on anything blocking you

**Minimum viable day = 3 real conversations + 3 logs.** Depth beats count — one owner who tells you
the truth about his test papers is worth five polite hellos.

`references/08_TEAM_BUILD_PLAYBOOK.md` — who to hire, how to pay them, how to run the week, and how
