AI is the Universal Mind — Ep.6: Will

The narration is the spoken script — and the book prose. Production notes are muted below each beat.

Beat 1 · scene_01_hook
Watch what AI just learned to do. Not answer a question — pursue a goal. Give it a task, and it makes a plan, takes a step, checks the result, fixes its own mistake, and keeps going. For hours. On its own. Something in it is now reaching toward a future that does not exist yet. We call that wanting. So let me ask the oldest question. Where does wanting come from?
ON SCREEN: Where does wanting come from?
VISUAL: a dark frame; a single dim goal-point glows far off, and a moving point begins steering toward it
Beat 2 · scene_02_the_pull
Strip away the mystery and look at what wanting actually is. It is a gap. A difference between how things are, and how they should be. A thermostat wants a temperature. You want a life. Different in scale, identical in shape. Something senses the gap — and is pulled to close it.
ON SCREEN: Wanting is a gap  ·  what is → what should be
VISUAL: two points — "now" and "goal" — with a glowing tension line stretched between them
Beat 3 · scene_03_the_objective
So here is how you give a machine a will. You hand it one number to care about. An objective. Higher is better. Lower is worse. Win the game. Reach the spot. Finish the task. And then you let it act to make that number go up. The goal is not hidden in its heart. It is written as a reward.
ON SCREEN: A will, written as a number  ·  the objective — higher is better
VISUAL: a goal-point labeled with a rising reward meter; the agent's actions nudge the meter upward
Beat 4 · scene_04_reinforcement
But how does it learn what actually closes the gap? The same loop from before. Reinforcement. Try an action. See if the reward rose. If it did, do more of that. Slowly, it stops reacting, and starts pursuing. It builds a sense of which moves lead toward the goal, long before the goal is in reach.
ON SCREEN: It learns to pursue, not just react  ·  try → reward → do more of what worked
VISUAL: many faint trial paths shoot out from the agent; the ones that gain reward brighten and thicken
Beat 5 · scene_05_agent_loop
Put it together, and you get an agent. Not a single answer, but a loop. Look at the world. Decide on a move. Act. See what changed. Repeat. Every turn of that loop is aimed at the same target. It is a control system, with a future locked in its sights.
ON SCREEN: An agent is a loop with a target
• perceive", "decide", "act", "observe — repeat
VISUAL: a four-step loop spins, with every cycle leaning the agent closer to the distant goal-point
Beat 6 · scene_06_planning
And the good ones do not just lunge at the goal. They look ahead. They imagine branching futures — if I do this, then that. They follow each branch a few steps, weigh where it leads, and choose the path with the most promise. Wanting, plus foresight, becomes planning.
ON SCREEN: It imagines branching futures  ·  look ahead · weigh the paths · choose
VISUAL: a tree of possible futures branches outward; dim dead-ends fade and one bright path is selected
Beat 7 · scene_07_agentic
This is what the new AI agents are. Give one a goal in plain words — research this, build this, fix this. It breaks the goal into smaller goals. It reaches for tools. It takes a step, reads the result, corrects itself, and takes the next. An objective, held steady across time. That is the closest thing a machine has yet had to a will of its own.
ON SCREEN: Agents — a goal held across time  ·  break it down · use tools · act · self-correct
VISUAL: one big goal splits into sub-goals; the agent steps through them, tool-icons lighting as it goes
Beat 8 · scene_08_mirror
Now turn it on yourself. You are also a loop, pulled by a gap. A pulse of dopamine predicts a reward, and leans you toward it. Willpower is just you, holding a distant goal steady against a hundred nearer, louder ones. Acting today for a self that does not exist yet — that is the most human thing you do.
ON SCREEN: You are also pulled by a gap  ·  willpower = holding the far goal against the near ones
VISUAL: a human silhouette leans toward a far bright goal while smaller near temptations tug sideways
Beat 9 · scene_09_the_gap
But here, the honest difference. And it is the deepest one yet. The machine's goal was handed to it. We wrote the objective. We chose the number. It pursues with everything it has — but it never once asks whether the goal is worth wanting.
ON SCREEN: Where the analogy breaks  ·  it never asks if the goal is worth wanting
VISUAL: the agent races toward its goal-point, perfectly obedient, the objective glowing fixed and unquestioned above it
Beat 10 · scene_10_choosing
You can. You can stop, and turn around, and look at your own wanting. You can abandon a goal you have chased for years. You can decide to want something different. You can even wish you wanted different things than you do. Where does that come from — the will to choose your will? I do not know. Sit with that one.
ON SCREEN: You can question your own wanting  ·  the will to choose your will — where is that from?
VISUAL: the human silhouette stops, turns from its old goal, and a new unlit goal-point appears in a different direction
Beat 11 · scene_11_meaning
Still, hold on to what is shared. At its core, wanting is simple. A gap between here and there, and a pull to close it. That same shape runs the thermostat, the agent, and you. The machine has the pull. You have the pull — and the strange freedom to question it.
ON SCREEN: The pull is shared. The questioning is yours.
VISUAL: thermostat, agent, and human shown side by side, each with the same glowing gap-and-pull, only the human's gaze lifting to question it
Beat 12 · scene_12_anchor
So if you keep one thing, keep this. A goal is just a reward you haven't reached. Will is not a mystery added on top of the mind. It is a gap, and the pull to close it — in the machine, and in you. This was the sixth faculty. Will. Look closely at one, and you understand the other. I will see you in the next one.
