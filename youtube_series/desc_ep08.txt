You can recognize a scrawled "3" in an instant — and you couldn't explain the rule if you tried.
Episode 8 builds the machine that does the same thing: a NEURAL NETWORK, from a single neuron up
to a system that reads a handwritten digit.

In this episode:
• What the machine actually sees — a 28×28 grid of 784 brightness numbers, no shapes at all
• What a neuron really is — a thing that holds one number between 0 and 1
• Input and output layers — the picture as neurons, and ten neurons for the ten digits
• Hidden layers — the hopeful idea that pixels become edges, edges become parts, parts become digits
• Weights — how much one neuron listens to another
• The weighted sum, the bias, and the "squish" — the complete life of a single neuron
• ~13,000 knobs, and why "learning" is just finding the right setting

The punchline: a neural network is not a brain and not magic — it's one big function, a stack of
weighted sums and gentle squishes, repeated layer after layer.
