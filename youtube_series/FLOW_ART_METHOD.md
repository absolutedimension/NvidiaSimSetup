## The one line

## The method, as you actually do it (your words, arranged)

### 1. The two body templates — the trained patterns (built *before* flow)
Through long exposure to dance music, your body learned **motor templates** that *capture the
beat* without thought:
- **Template A — full body (house / shuffle style):** legs + hands. Standing, moving.
- **Template B — minimal (hands + fingers):** capture the beat seated, no leg movement.

These aren't choreography. They're **trained policies** — patterns wired in by repetition, so
the body can take the beat while *you* are absent.

### 2. The recurring beat — the door
You find the beat that **comes back periodically** in the rhythm, and you use *that* beat as
your point of entry. (The recurring beat is the most *predictable* event — so it's the
strongest, most reliable door to drop through.)

### 3. The losing — the core skill (this is the whole art)
On that recurring beat, you **lose.** Not "relax" — *lose*: no longer aware of the dance, "now
nothing at all." The observer — the part that watches *"how do I look while dancing"* — goes
silent. The moment it does, you *see your body catching the rhythm by itself.* The template
runs; you are not steering it.

### 4. Silence to silence — how a long dance sustains
You don't hold flow continuously. A long dance is **moving from one losing to the next** —
re-entering on each recurring beat. That's why there's no effort: you're not *maintaining*
anything, you're *re-dropping* through the door again and again.

### 5. The law of effort (the paradox)
- **You don't *try* to dance. You find the point of losing.** You don't worry about the steps.
- **The more you don't worry, the better it gets.**
- The instant you become *conscious*, you come out of the dance — or you start spending energy.
- So: **effort = exit + drain. Surrender = stay + gain.**

### 6. The beginner's obstacle
The first wall is **self-image** — the beginner is aware of being looked at, stuck in the
point of view of *how he looks.* That observer-self is the thing that must dissolve first.
