## The problem

Filming a live performance with a drone requires a skilled pilot AND a creative
cinematographer — two rare, expensive humans working in perfect sync. Autonomous
drones today (DJI, Skydio) can follow a subject, but they orbit in fixed circles.
They don't know what a "hero shot" is. They don't vary framing. They don't time a
push-in to the peak of a spin.

**Result:** Drone footage of performances looks generic — same distance, same angle,
same energy for 25 minutes straight.

---

## What we do

We train a **neural network cinematographer** specifically for your performance —
and your director controls it by voice in real-time.

**6 cinematic modes, voice-directed on set:**

| Mode | What it shoots |
|---|---|
| **HERO** | Low-angle power shots — camera below waist, looking up |
| **INTIMATE** | Close framing, face-level, portrait feel |
| **EPIC** | Wide establishing shots, bird's-eye crane moments |
| **ENERGY** | Fast-paced movement synced to the music's beat |
| **SOLITUDE** | Isolation framing — subject small in the frame, negative space |
| **BEAUTY** | Balanced composition, smooth orbits, rule-of-thirds |

**Real-time voice direction:** Your director says "hero" — the drone shifts to
low-angle power shots within 2 seconds. "Closer." "Slower." "Switch to epic."
"Hold." The director stays in creative control. The drone provides execution no
human pilot can match consistently.

**90-second venue setup:** Fly the drone to the corners of your stage, press MARK.
No GPS. No pre-mapping. The drone defines its own safe boundary and never crosses it.

The policy runs at 50 Hz — reacting to your performer's movement in real-time.
It's not a pre-planned path. It's a directed creative intelligence.
